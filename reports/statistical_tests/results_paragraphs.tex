Table~\ref{tab:results-main} compares LGCP against all five baselines. To assess statistical significance, we compare LGCP with each baseline using a paired, two-sided exact Wilcoxon signed-rank test over the eight test weeks (four per month; stochastic methods averaged over seeds), with Holm correction across the five baselines for each metric (App.~\ref{app:significance}). LGCP achieves the lowest MAE in both months, and its MAE is significantly lower than that of every baseline: it is lower in all eight weeks for four of them and in seven of eight weeks for ConvLSTM (Holm-adjusted $p=0.039$ for every baseline, the smallest value attainable with eight weeks and five baselines). LGCP also achieves the best Wasserstein distance among all genuinely predictive models (i.e.\ excluding Last Available Poisson, whose near-zero Wasserstein is a trivial artifact of copying real observed counts rather than a sign of good calibration), outperforming each of them in at least seven of the eight weeks ($p=0.039$). Most strikingly, LGCP's accuracy and F1 (0.89--0.90 and 0.58--0.63) are far ahead of every other covariate-based baseline, which all obtain identical scores (0.12--0.13 accuracy and 0.22--0.23 F1) regardless of architecture (GLM, GNN, or ConvLSTM); LGCP outperforms each of them, as well as Global Cell Mean Poisson, in all eight weeks on both metrics ($p=0.039$). Its F1 is also significantly higher than that of Last Available Poisson (eight of eight weeks, $p=0.039$), whose accuracy it exceeds in six of eight weeks ($p=0.055$). As the ablation in App.~\ref{app:ablation} confirms, this gap is driven entirely by the zero-inflation classifier rather than by the kernel or the choice of count model, since none of the other baselines include an analogous activity-detection mechanism and so default to predicting a strictly positive rate everywhere.

ConvLSTM and GNN are the baselines closest to LGCP in a pointwise sense: they achieve the best and second-best RMSE in both months, and MAEs close to, though significantly higher than, that of LGCP. This reflects a genuine trade-off rather than a shortcoming of LGCP, since RMSE weights large individual errors more heavily than MAE, while LGCP's zero-gate is tuned to get the common case (and the activity/inactivity decision) right rather than to minimize worst-case error on the occasional large vessel count; indeed, none of the RMSE differences between LGCP and the baselines is statistically significant (unadjusted $p\ge0.19$). Window Poisson GLM is the weakest covariate-based model, with the highest MAE and RMSE of all methods in both months.
