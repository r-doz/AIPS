## Exploratory Spatio-Temporal Dependence Analysis

We performed an exploratory spatio-temporal dependence analysis on the full 2024 dataset in order to better understand the empirical structure of the target variable before further modifying the LGCP kernel. This analysis was not used as a test-set evaluation, but as a diagnostic step to characterize the temporal, spatial and spatio-temporal dependence of `ais_vessels_count`.

The dataset contains 17,934 observations, 366 dates and 49 spatial cells. The target is highly sparse: approximately 90.7% of the observation-level values are zero. This sparsity is important when interpreting correlation and variogram results, especially at the single-cell level.

First, we computed the autocorrelation of the daily total number of vessels. This revealed a strong temporal structure at the aggregate level. The daily totals showed high autocorrelation at lag 1 and a particularly strong signal at lag 7, with additional positive correlation at lags 14 and 21. This suggests that the total daily activity has a clear temporal pattern, possibly related to weekly operational cycles, calendar effects, weather conditions or fishing activity patterns. This result supports the idea that the model should include a temporal component capable of capturing persistence over several days, rather than only one-day dependence.

Second, we computed temporal autocorrelation separately for each spatial cell and summarized the results across cells. The cell-level autocorrelation was weaker than the daily-total autocorrelation. The median correlation was positive at lag 1 and showed a secondary increase around lag 7, but values were generally much lower than in the aggregate daily series. This indicates that temporal dependence is present at the cell level, but it is noisier and more sparse. In other words, the phenomenon has a stronger temporal signal at the global daily level than at the individual grid-cell level.

Third, we computed a spatial variogram using both `y` and `log1p(y)`. The spatial variogram showed lower semivariance at short distances and a rapid increase within the first few kilometres, suggesting the presence of local spatial correlation. After approximately 5–10 km, the semivariance became relatively flat, indicating that spatial similarity decreases quickly with distance. The decrease observed at the largest distance bins should not be interpreted as a true long-range spatial structure, because the pair-count diagnostic showed that the last bins contain far fewer spatial pairs and are therefore less reliable.

Fourth, we computed a spatio-temporal variogram for multiple temporal lags. The curves for different lags were relatively close to each other, especially for `log1p(y)`. This suggests that, while both spatial and temporal dependence exist, the spatio-temporal variogram does not provide strong evidence for a highly complex non-separable kernel at this stage. The temporal structure appears more clearly in the daily aggregate series than in the full space-time variogram.

Overall, the diagnostics suggest the following interpretation. The data have a strong global temporal component, a weaker but still present cell-level temporal dependence, and a local spatial dependence structure. This is consistent with the empirical LGCP results: a temporal RBF kernel with lengthscale around `0.10`, corresponding to approximately 9–10 days in the original time scale, provided the best compromise among the tested configurations. Very short temporal lengthscales were stable but too conservative, while longer lengthscales propagated information too broadly and produced larger daily errors.

Based on these diagnostics, the current kernel structure remains reasonable. In particular, the spatial component `RBF + Rational Quadratic` is supported by the presence of local spatial dependence and possible multi-scale spatial variability, while the temporal component should remain an RBF kernel with an intermediate lengthscale around `0.10`. The annual periodic kernel should remain disabled for the single-year dataset, especially because the previous time-scaler diagnostic showed that `period = 1.0` corresponds to approximately 95 days in standardized time, not one year. A proper annual periodic component may become more meaningful when using a two-year dataset.

The current recommended LGCP configuration is therefore: temporal RBF only, temporal lengthscale fixed at `0.10`, temporal variance trainable, spatial RBF plus Rational Quadratic, and kernel noise fixed at `1e-3`. Future work may revisit more complex kernel structures, such as sums of space-time products, after extending the dataset to 2024–2025 or after further evidence that the separable kernel is insufficient.
